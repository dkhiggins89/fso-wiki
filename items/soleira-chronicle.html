A Ledger of Roads and Roofs

The old clerks of Castamar recorded wars by the arrows they spent and peace by the roofs they repaired. Their finest ledger has more entries for wells, bridges and market stalls than for battles. Duchess Leonor has ordered that it remain on the public shelf.

Corcova's cork travels farther than many of its cutters. It leaves the shaded groves in rough carts and returns as a stopper in a sailor's bottle. At Alvarena, the storks come home to the same chimneys, as if the white houses were harbour lights visible from the clouds.

Puerto Dorado keeps the final page of every year's account: ships arrived, ships departed, and names still awaited. The harbourmaster leaves a little room beneath the last line. The sea, he says, has never agreed to keep a clerk's hours.
