Townsfolk who look about and walk

2026-10-31

Until now every townsperson stood on one spot looking straight at the camera. Each NPC placed on a map can now do one of four things:

  • Stand still facing north, east, south or west: a guard can face down the road, a shopkeeper can face their

counter.

  • Turn on the spot now and then, between the directions chosen for them: a lookout scanning the street.
  • Walk a set route between chosen points, either back and forth or round in a loop, pausing at each point: a

watchman on patrol, a farmhand walking between the barn and the field.

  • Wander freely around where they were placed, a few steps at a time.

Around the towns you will notice:

  • Townsfolk, farmhands, herders and dock workers wander a few steps around their spot.
  • Guards on their own walk a short beat back and forth. Gate guards in pairs look out along the road, and the

town criers of Hohenburg and Castamar walk across their squares.

  • Priests, brothers and sisters turn between you and the altar. Mayors, harbourmasters and fishers look about.
  • Shopkeepers, innkeepers, smiths, captains and coachmen stay where they are, so you always know where to find

them, and nobody wanders up to stand in front of a counter.

Walking NPCs never step onto you or anyone else. If you stand in their way they wait until you move. They keep clear of doorways, so they never block a door. NPCs on the screens next to yours move too.

Any NPC, standing or walking, turns to face you when you talk to them. They stay put while you talk or trade, then go back to what they were doing. If you pick Talk-to on someone who is walking, you follow them until you are next to them.

With the smooth graphics each NPC shows its own front, side or back view. With the Original graphics they keep their one picture.

World builders set all of this per NPC in the map editor; see Making NPCs move.
